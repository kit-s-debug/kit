# Last Known Position — Channel Strategy & Production System

A complete operating system for a cinematic faceless documentary channel about strange real
events, unexplained mysteries, bizarre history, unusual places and disappearances.

Built for heavy AI assistance in research, scripting, visuals and narration, with the
editorial and craft discipline that stops the output reading as AI content.

## The short version

**Name:** Last Known Position — the search-and-rescue term for the final confirmed location
before contact was lost. It describes the format rather than the topic, so it covers
disappearances, places, objects and signals without locking you in.

**Positioning:** the mystery channel that tells you what it doesn't know. The whole
competitive gap right now is that this niche is loud, padded and unsourced. Restraint,
primary documents and an explicit line between record and theory is the differentiator, and
it is the thing this audience is most starved of.

**Format:** 11–14 minutes, 1,700–2,200 words, one video every Friday, four in the bank
before launch. One 25–35 minute flagship per quarter.

**Structure:** Hook → Setup → Rupture → Escalation ×3 → Revelation → Ending. Fixed, every
time, so retention graphs become comparable and diagnosable.

**Visuals:** 25% AI, 25% archival, 15% documents, 15% maps, 10% type, 10% texture. Never
more than two AI shots in a row. Never a generated close-up face. One grade, one grain, one
amber accent over everything.

**First five videos, in order:** the Ghost Blimp (L-8, 1942) · Roopkund skeleton lake ·
Vozrozhdeniya Island · the Hollow Nickel · the Circleville Letters.

**Target:** 50,000 long-form views. In this niche watch hours and subscribers arrive
together, so that single number clears both monetisation thresholds. Realistically 4–9
months and 13–25 videos.

**The contract:** thirteen videos before you are allowed to conclude anything.
