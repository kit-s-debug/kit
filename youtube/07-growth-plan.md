# 07 — Zero to 1,000 Subscribers

An honest plan. No growth hacks, because there aren't any that work in this niche without
poisoning the audience you actually want.

---

## 1. What the target actually is

Monetisation requires **1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 valid public watch hours in the
preceding twelve months.** There is also a lower tier at **500 subscribers, 3,000 watch
hours and three public uploads in the last 90 days**, which unlocks memberships, Super
Thanks and Super Chat in eligible countries. Hit that first; treat it as the real halfway
marker, because it is the first point at which the channel can earn anything at all.

**The maths that makes this plan tractable:**

- 4,000 hours = 240,000 minutes.
- A 12-minute video watched at 50% average = 6 minutes per view.
- 240,000 ÷ 6 = **40,000 views.**

Subscriber conversion for well-made documentary content in this niche typically runs
1.5–3% of views — higher than most genres, because "I want more of exactly this" is the
dominant reaction to a good episode.

- 40,000 views × 2% = **800 subscribers.**
- 50,000 views × 2% = **1,000 subscribers.**

**The important consequence: in this niche, watch hours and subscribers arrive at
roughly the same moment.** You do not need two strategies. Optimise for one number —
total minutes watched — and both thresholds fall together. Any tactic that raises views
but not watch time (Shorts spam, clickbait, cross-posting to disinterested audiences)
actively makes the target harder, not easier.

**The single target: 50,000 long-form views.**

---

## 2. The realistic shape of it

Most channels in this niche that get there do it like this: eight to fifteen videos of
modest performance while the recommendation system works out who the audience is, and then
one video that breaks out and drags everything behind it.

You cannot schedule the breakout. You can only make sure there are enough well-packaged
episodes in the library for the algorithm to have something to test, and that when one
does break, the next-best video is right there in the end screen.

A plausible distribution across your first 20 videos: eleven or twelve at 300–1,500 views,
five or six at 2,000–6,000, one or two at 15,000–60,000. The breakout does most of the
work. Everything before it is you buying lottery tickets with quality.

**Expect 4–9 months.** Anyone promising faster is selling something.

### Milestones to judge yourself against

| Point | On track | Struggling | Action if struggling |
|-------|----------|-----------|---------------------|
| **Video 5** | 150–400 subs, one video over 1,000 views, CTR 3.5%+ | Under 60 subs, no video over 400 views | Packaging problem. Rewrite all five titles and thumbnails before making video 6 |
| **Video 10** | 400–900 subs, 8,000+ total views, AVD 45%+ | Under 200 subs, AVD under 35% | Retention problem. Read every graph against the diagnostic table; the hooks or the Setup beat are wrong |
| **Video 13** | 700–1,500 subs, approaching or past monetisation | Under 350 subs | Topic-selection problem. Your scoring is too generous on curiosity gap. Only build 20+ scores for the next five |
| **Video 20** | Monetised, one clear breakout, returning-viewer share climbing | Still under 600 | Re-examine whether the niche framing is too narrow, and repackage the best three videos |

## 3. Before you publish anything

**Do not launch until all of this exists.**

- [ ] Four finished videos in the bank
- [ ] Channel name, handle, `.com`, and matching handles on Instagram, TikTok and X
- [ ] Avatar (the mark), banner, and a channel description written as a *promise*, not a
      list of topics — e.g. *"Reconstructions of real events from the documents. Every
      claim sourced. When nobody knows, we say so."*
- [ ] Thumbnail template, title-card template and NLE project template built
- [ ] Two playlists named as series, not as categories
- [ ] The style reference image locked, so episode 1 and episode 40 look related
- [ ] A `sources` block format decided for descriptions

**No channel trailer.** Pin your best video instead. Trailers underperform for
documentary channels because the audience arrives from a specific video already interested
in a specific story.

## 4. The four things that actually move the number

Ranked by return on effort. Do these; treat everything else as optional.

### 4.1 Repackage relentlessly — highest return, lowest effort

Your back catalogue is inventory, not history.

- At 48 hours, 7 days and 30 days, check click-through rate. Below ~4% from browse and
  suggested means the packaging is the bottleneck, not the film.
- Swap in one of the other two thumbnails you already made. Change one variable at a time.
- Rewrite titles on older videos when you learn something. A video from month one, retitled
  in month six with everything you have learned, is the cheapest new video you will ever
  make.
- Track every change in the production database with the before/after CTR.

This one habit is responsible for more small-channel breakouts than any other single
practice, and it costs fifteen minutes a week.

### 4.2 Fix retention, video by video

CTR gets people in. Retention decides whether the algorithm shows the video to anyone else.

- Target: **75%+ still watching at 0:30**, and **45–55% average percentage viewed**.
- Read the graph at 48 hours against the diagnostic table in `02-content-formula.md`.
- **Change exactly one thing per video** and write it down. Ten videos of tracked
  single-variable changes teaches you more about your specific audience than any amount of
  general advice.
- The two highest-leverage fixes, almost always: a hook that starts inside a moment rather
  than with context, and a Setup beat that delivers stakes rather than background.

### 4.3 Build sessions, not views

YouTube rewards the *session*, not the video. A viewer who watches two of your videos
back-to-back is worth far more than two viewers who watch one each.

- Choose the end-screen video deliberately for every upload. Pick the one whose thumbnail
  pairs best with the mood the viewer is in at the end of *this* film, not the newest one.
- Put every video in a named series playlist and set the playlist to autoplay.
- Pin a comment linking the single most related episode, phrased as a genuine
  recommendation with a reason.
- Once you have eight videos, make an ordered "start here" playlist and link it in the
  banner.

### 4.4 Reply to every comment for 48 hours

For the first six months, answer every single comment within the first two days.

It is not about the algorithm. It is that a hundred-view video with fifteen real replies
from you builds a group of people who come back for video two, and video two is where
channels either start or die. Those first two hundred people are the entire asset.

Pin a comment with a real extra fact and its source. It signals that the research went
deeper than the runtime, which is exactly the reputation this channel wants.

## 5. Cold-start distribution, done honestly

Most communities where this content lands ban self-promotion, and they are right to. The
strategy that works is **post the artefact, not the link.**

For every video, produce one asset that stands alone:

- The map you built, exported as a clean image
- A comparison of two photographs
- A scan of the key document with the relevant line highlighted
- A single well-chosen archival photograph with a two-paragraph caption

Post that natively — as an image, in the community it fits, with the story told properly
in the post itself and your sources in a comment. People who want more will find the
channel. People who wanted a video link were never going to watch twelve minutes anyway.

**Where:** map and geography communities for the map assets; history and archive
communities for the documents; abandoned-places communities for the location episodes;
general "today I learned" style communities for the strongest single facts — always
linking the primary source, never your video.

**Read and follow each community's rules before posting.** One ban costs you more than the
traffic was ever worth, and this audience notices and dislikes people who spam.

**What not to do:** buy anything, join engagement pods, sub-for-sub, or post links where
they aren't wanted. Beyond being against platform policy, artificially inflated views from
uninterested people wreck your retention and CTR signals, and those signals are the only
thing deciding whether YouTube shows your video to the people who would have loved it.

## 6. Slate strategy: search versus browse

Roughly **one in three videos should target existing search demand.** Something people are
already typing in: a named case with recognition, a well-known place, an event that
resurfaces periodically.

Those videos accumulate views slowly and permanently, giving you a floor while you wait for
a browse hit. The other two in three are pure browse plays — the "how have I never heard of
this" stories that live or die on packaging but have far higher ceilings.

From the slate in `06-video-slate.md`: the Max Headroom intrusion, Lake Nyos, Roopkund and
the Yuba County Five carry the most existing search demand. The Ghost Blimp, the Hollow
Nickel, Vozrozhdeniya, Tromelin and the Bell Island Boom are browse plays. Alternate them.

## 7. The first thirteen weeks

**Weeks −4 to 0 (build):** template library, brand assets, videos 1–4 finished, channel
page complete.

**Week 1:** Publish video 1 (Ghost Blimp). Do not tell anyone it is a "new channel" — no
launch announcement, no "please support my new project." The video either works or it
doesn't. Reply to every comment.

**Weeks 2–4:** Videos 2–4. Maintain the buffer by finishing one a week. First repackaging
pass on video 1.

**Week 5 — first review.** Look at CTR, 30-second retention and AVD across four videos.
Identify which single beat is losing the most people. Change that one thing.

**Weeks 6–9:** Videos 5–8. Start posting artefacts. Build the "start here" playlist.

**Week 9 — second review.** By now you should be able to name your best-performing topic
*category*, not just video. Weight the next block toward it.

**Weeks 10–13:** Videos 9–12. Introduce Shorts if long-form is healthy. First quarterly
flagship goes into research.

**Week 13 — the honest assessment.** Thirteen videos is the minimum sample for a real
judgement. Before that, everything is noise. Use the milestone table above.

**The contract with yourself: thirteen videos before you are allowed to conclude anything.**
The single most common way channels of this kind fail is changing direction at video five,
then again at video nine, so the recommendation system never learns who to show them to.

## 8. Beyond 1,000

Written down now so the early decisions point the right way.

- **Ad revenue is the floor, not the plan.** In this demographic it is real money at scale
  but it is not a business at 20,000 subscribers.
- **The first paid upgrade is a human narrator.** It is the biggest single quality jump
  available and it removes the last obvious AI tell.
- **The second is a researcher.** Research is the most delegable stage and the one most
  responsible for episode quality — buy hours there before buying editing hours.
- **Sponsorship** becomes available around 10–20k subscribers in this niche and pays
  substantially better than ads. Place reads after the Revelation beat, never before it,
  and never take a sponsor whose product contradicts the channel's epistemic promise.
- **Memberships** work for this audience specifically if what they buy is *the research*:
  the source documents, the claims sheet, the cut material. That is a real product for
  exactly the kind of person who watches this channel, and it costs you nothing because
  you already made all of it.
- **The flagship documentaries are the long game.** A 35-minute film every quarter is what
  turns a YouTube channel into something people describe as a studio.
