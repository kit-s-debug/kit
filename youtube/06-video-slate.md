# 06 — The Video Slate

Twenty commissioned stories, packaged. All are real, documented events with traceable
primary sources. Every one still requires the full Stage 1–3 research and fact-check
process before scripting — the notes here are commissioning briefs, not verified copy.

**Packaging tests every title must pass:**

1. **Curiosity, not concealment.** The title states a real, strange fact and stops. It does
   not hide the subject to force a click.
2. **It must be true after watching.** If a viewer finishes and feels the title oversold,
   you have lost them permanently.
3. **Under 60 characters** where possible, so it doesn't truncate on mobile.
4. **A concrete noun in the first three words.** "The blimp," "the lake," "the nickel."
5. **No question mark unless the video seriously attempts the answer.**
6. **Say it out loud.** If it sounds like a YouTube title, rewrite it. If it sounds like a
   thing a person would say to a friend, keep it.

**Thumbnail rules:** one dominant image · 3–5 words maximum in Archivo Bold bone · the amber
accent on exactly one element, marking the anomaly · no faces reacting · no red arrows · no
outlined heads · heavy vignette · legible at 210×118px.

# PART ONE — The five strongest

Ranked on curiosity gap, source depth, visual availability, competition and ad safety.
These five should be videos 1–5, in this order.

## ★ 1. The Ghost Blimp — L-8, 1942

**Premise:** A US Navy airship on a routine wartime patrol drifted back over San Francisco
and landed in a suburban street with everything aboard intact and both crewmen gone, and the
Navy's inquiry never established how they left.

**Why it's first:** It is the perfect opening episode. One unforgettable image with real
photographs behind it. A scene that is strange purely as an *inventory* — nothing gruesome,
nothing to soften. A formal Navy board of inquiry means genuine primary documents.
Abundant WWII archival film. Completely ad-safe. And almost nobody has told it well.

**Titles**

1. The Navy Airship That Landed With Nobody Aboard
2. In 1942, a US Navy Blimp Came Home Empty
3. The Ghost Blimp of Daly City
4. Two Men Vanished From a Sealed Airship. It Flew Home Without Them.
5. The L-8 Landed in a Suburban Street. The Crew Were Gone.

**★ Recommended:** *In 1942, a US Navy Blimp Came Home Empty*

Fifty characters. A date to anchor it as real history, a concrete object in the first four
words, and "came home empty" is plain language that implies everything without a single
dramatic adjective. Title 4 tests well too but "vanished" is the most exhausted word in the
niche.

**Thumbnail:** The real photograph of L-8 collapsed between two houses on Bellevue Avenue —
the enormous grey envelope draped over a residential street, absurdly out of scale with the
parked cars. Desaturated hard. An amber circle around the open, empty gondola door. Text
bottom-left.

**Thumbnail text:** `THE CREW WERE GONE`

**Opening hook:**

> At 6:03 on the morning of the sixteenth of August, 1942, a US Navy airship lifted off from
> Treasure Island in San Francisco Bay. Two men were aboard. It was an anti-submarine
> patrol — a slow circuit over the water, the kind they had flown dozens of times.
>
> Four hours later the airship came down in a residential street in Daly City. A fire crew
> climbed into the gondola.
>
> The radio worked. The life raft was still stowed. The parachutes had not been touched.
>
> Both men were gone. [hold]

**Length:** 12 minutes

**Research notes:** Navy board of inquiry records; contemporary San Francisco press;
the crew's names and service records; the documented radio message about an oil slick;
what the inventory did and did not list. Be careful — several widely-repeated details
about this case trace to later retellings rather than the inquiry. Establish which is which
and make that distinction part of the third act.

## ★ 2. Roopkund — The Lake of Bones

**Premise:** Hundreds of human skeletons lie in a glacial lake five kilometres up in the
Indian Himalaya, and when geneticists finally sequenced them in 2019 the answer was far
stranger than a single disaster.

**Why it's high:** The reveal is genuinely one of the best in the genre — for seventy years
everyone assumed one catastrophe, and the DNA showed something nobody had considered. Real
photographs of the site are extraordinary and instantly thumbnail-able. It is anchored in
peer-reviewed science, which makes it unimpeachable. And it ends with a question the science
made *bigger*, not smaller.

**Titles**

1. A Lake of Human Bones, 5,000 Metres Up
2. The Skeleton Lake, and the DNA Result Nobody Expected
3. Roopkund: 500 Skeletons and Three Different Answers
4. They Sequenced the Bones in the Himalayan Lake. It Made No Sense.
5. The Bodies in the Frozen Lake Weren't From the Same Century

**★ Recommended:** *The Bodies in the Frozen Lake Weren't From the Same Century*

It gives away the shape of the twist without giving away the twist, which is exactly the
right amount. It reframes something the viewer thought they understood in the first six
words, and it is a true statement — the film delivers precisely this.

**Thumbnail:** The real photograph of scattered skeletal remains at the lake edge, shot from
above, ice and grey scree, no close human detail. An amber survey circle around one
cluster. Cold, wide, high-altitude light.

**Thumbnail text:** `SAME LAKE. DIFFERENT CENTURIES.`

**Opening hook:**

> Roopkund is a small lake in the Indian Himalaya, about five thousand metres above sea
> level. For most of the year it is frozen solid. For a few weeks in summer, the ice
> retreats, and you can see the bottom.
>
> There are hundreds of people down there.
>
> Not bodies — bones. Skulls, femurs, whole articulated skeletons, some still with hair and
> fragments of clothing, spread across the shallows and the shoreline.
>
> For seventy years the question was what killed them all at once. In 2019 a genetics team
> answered it. And the answer was that nothing did. [hold]

**Length:** 13 minutes

**Research notes:** Harney et al. 2019 in *Nature Communications* is the spine of the film.
Read the paper itself, not the press coverage. The genetically distinct groups, the
radiocarbon separation of several centuries, and the eastern-Mediterranean-ancestry group
are the three reveals. The local folk ballad about a doomed royal party is a beautiful
Act I and must be labelled `REPORTED`, never presented as history.

## ★ 3. Vozrozhdeniya — The Island That Stopped Being an Island

**Premise:** The Soviet Union built its main biological weapons test site on an island in the
middle of the Aral Sea, buried its anthrax stockpile there in 1988, and then the sea dried up
and the island joined the mainland.

**Why it's high:** The "how have I never heard of this" quotient is enormous. Declassified
material and serious journalism give it real sourcing. And it has the best visual sequence
available to this channel: forty years of satellite imagery showing a sea disappearing and an
island becoming a peninsula, animated as a single continuous shot. That sequence alone is
worth the episode.

**Titles**

1. The Island That Stopped Being an Island
2. The Soviet Bioweapons Island the Aral Sea Uncovered
3. They Buried It on an Island. Then the Sea Dried Up.
4. Kantubek: The Town That Was Never on Any Map
5. The Aral Sea Vanished, and Something Was Waiting Underneath

**★ Recommended:** *They Buried It on an Island. Then the Sea Dried Up.*

Two short sentences that create the entire premise and the entire dread in eleven words. It
withholds the subject noun, but the thumbnail supplies it — the title and image work as a
pair, which is how packaging should be built.

**Thumbnail:** A satellite composite — the Aral Sea's 1985 coastline as a bone-coloured
outline over the dried 2020 seabed, with the island shape now clearly connected to land.
Amber marker and grid coordinates on the island. No text on the image itself except the
overlay line.

**Thumbnail text:** `THE SEA WAS THE ONLY BARRIER`

**Opening hook:**

> In 1988, a convoy of trucks drove out onto a remote island in the middle of the Aral Sea,
> carrying several hundred tonnes of a bacterial slurry in steel drums. The drums were
> lowered into pits. The pits were filled in.
>
> The island had been chosen decades earlier for one reason. It was surrounded, on every
> side, by seventy kilometres of open water. Nothing could walk off it.
>
> Fourteen years later, you could. [hold]

**Length:** 14 minutes

**Research notes:** Aralsk-7 and the town of Kantubek; the 2002 US-funded decontamination
operation; Ken Alibek's testimony and the caveats that attach to a single defector source;
the sequence of Aral Sea shrinkage from irrigation diversion. Distinguish carefully between
documented programme facts, defector testimony, and speculation about what remains. The
`THEORY` and `UNKNOWN` tags will get a lot of use here — which is exactly the point.

## ★ 4. The Hollow Nickel

**Premise:** In 1953 a Brooklyn newspaper boy dropped a nickel on the pavement and it split
open, and the microfilm inside took the FBI four years, a Soviet defector and a spy swap on a
Berlin bridge to explain.

**Why it's high:** A perfect story engine — a tiny domestic object that opens into the largest
possible frame. The escalation is built into the events themselves, which is rare. It is
extensively documented by the FBI, entirely ad-safe, visually rich (period Brooklyn, coded
message, the coin itself, the bridge), and it *resolves* — proving to your new audience that
this channel can deliver an answer, not just an atmosphere.

**Titles**

1. A Paperboy Dropped a Nickel. It Split in Half.
2. The Coin That Fell Apart on a Brooklyn Pavement
3. The Hollow Nickel: A Coded Message the FBI Couldn't Read for Four Years
4. It Took Four Years and a Defector to Read What Was Inside This Coin
5. The Nickel, the Microfilm, and the Man on the Bridge

**★ Recommended:** *A Paperboy Dropped a Nickel. It Split in Half.*

The most ordinary opening imaginable, ending on a physical impossibility. Forty-five
characters, two short sentences, and it makes the viewer need to know one specific thing.
It also does not sound like a spy story, which is why the story lands so hard.

**Thumbnail:** A macro photograph of a 1948 US nickel opened into two hollow halves against
black, one half tilted, a square of microfilm beside it. Amber rim-light on the cut edge.
Extremely high contrast, almost a product shot — which is what makes it stop the scroll in a
feed full of grey landscapes.

**Thumbnail text:** `INSIDE THE COIN`

**Opening hook:**

> In the summer of 1953, a fourteen-year-old boy was collecting subscription money on a
> street in Brooklyn. Two customers paid him in change. He dropped some of it on the
> pavement.
>
> One of the coins didn't sound right. He picked it up. It was too light.
>
> Then it came apart in his hand.
>
> Inside the nickel, folded into the hollow, was a square of microfilm. On the microfilm
> were two hundred and seven numbers, in groups of five. [beat] Nobody would be able to read
> them for four years. [hold]

**Length:** 12 minutes

**Research notes:** The FBI's own case file on the Hollow Nickel case is public and detailed.
Reino Häyhänen's defection and what he supplied; Rudolf Abel / Vilyam Fisher; the 1962
exchange at the Glienicke Bridge. The genuine open question worth ending on: the FBI held
that microfilm for four years without a key, and it is worth being honest about how much of
this was investigation and how much was luck.

## ★ 5. The Circleville Letters

**Premise:** From 1976, thousands of anonymous letters full of accurate private information
arrived in one small Ohio town, and they did not stop when the man convicted of writing them
went to prison — one arrived addressed to him, inside.

**Why it's high:** The strongest pure mystery in the slate, with an escalation that is almost
structurally perfect: letters, then a death, then a booby-trapped roadside sign built by
somebody who knew engineering, then a conviction, then the letters continuing. Genuinely
unsolved. Extensively documented in Ohio press and court records.

**Caution:** the case involves a death and an attempted shooting. Handle per the rules in
`03-scripting-standards.md` — clinical, non-graphic, no speculation about living named
individuals' guilt. Keep the sensitive terms out of the title, the thumbnail and the first
thirty seconds.

**Titles**

1. The Letters Kept Coming After He Went to Prison
2. Someone in Circleville Knew Everything
3. Thousands of Letters. One Small Town. No Answer.
4. The Circleville Letters: Ohio's Longest Unsolved Correspondence
5. He Was Convicted of Writing the Letters. Then He Received One.

**★ Recommended:** *He Was Convicted of Writing the Letters. Then He Received One.*

The single best curiosity gap in the whole slate. Two sentences, a complete reversal, and
every word of it is factually accurate. It also frames the video as a question about
evidence rather than a question about horror, which is the tone this channel wants.

**Thumbnail:** A plain handwritten envelope on a dark surface, addressed in block capitals,
postmark visible. Amber circle around the postmark date. That is the entire image — the
restraint is the point, and in a feed of grey landscape shots a single white envelope is
the most eye-catching thing on the page.

**Thumbnail text:** `HE WAS IN PRISON`

**Opening hook:**

> The letters started arriving in Circleville, Ohio, in March 1976. Block capitals. Ohio
> postmarks. No signature.
>
> They went to a school bus driver, and to her husband, and then to her employer, and then
> to people who had nothing to do with either of them. They contained accurate private
> details about people's lives — the kind of thing you would only know if you lived close
> enough to watch.
>
> Over the next fifteen years, the writer sent thousands of them.
>
> One of the last was delivered to a state prison, to the man who had already been convicted
> of writing them. [hold]

**Length:** 13 minutes

**Research notes:** Ohio newspaper archives from 1977 through the 1990s; the trial record for
the 1983 conviction; the postal inspection service's involvement. Be precise and conservative
about the death of Ron Gillispie and about the handwriting analysis, both of which are
contested. This episode lives or dies on the epistemic discipline.

## 6. The Joyita

**Premise:** A ship that was almost impossible to sink was found adrift in the Pacific in
1955, listing and half-flooded, with all twenty-five people aboard gone and no distress call
ever received.

**Titles:** 1. Twenty-Five People Left a Ship That Couldn't Sink · 2. The Joyita Was Found
Six Hundred Miles Off Course · 3. The Ship Stayed Afloat. Everyone Aboard Did Not. ·
4. A Cork-Lined Hull, a Broken Radio, and Twenty-Five Missing People · 5. Nobody Ever Found
the Joyita's Crew

**★ Recommended:** *Twenty-Five People Left a Ship That Couldn't Sink* — states the paradox
in nine words and makes the viewer supply the question themselves.

**Thumbnail:** The real 1955 photograph of Joyita listing heavily in the water, shot from a
boat. Amber outline tracing the waterline. Empty grey Pacific behind.

**Thumbnail text:** `SHE STAYED AFLOAT`

**Hook:** The Joyita was found on the tenth of November, drifting six hundred miles from
where she should have been, listing so far to port that the deck was awash. Twenty-five
people had boarded her. None of them were aboard. And the one thing everyone agreed on
afterwards was that this was a ship you had no reason to leave.

**Length:** 12 minutes

## 7. The Buzzer — UVB-76

**Premise:** A Russian shortwave frequency has broadcast a monotonous buzzing tone almost
continuously since the early 1980s, interrupted only by occasional voices reading lists of
words, and nobody outside Russia knows what it is for.

**Titles:** 1. The Radio Station That Has Been Buzzing Since 1982 · 2. 4625 kHz: Forty Years
of the Same Sound · 3. Somebody Is Still Maintaining This Signal · 4. The Buzzer Has Never
Stopped · 5. A Frequency That Says Almost Nothing, Forever

**★ Recommended:** *Somebody Is Still Maintaining This Signal* — shifts the mystery from the
sound to the people, which is the more unsettling frame and the more honest one.

**Thumbnail:** A close-up of an analogue radio dial in near-darkness, needle on 4625, amber
backlight bleeding through the glass. A waveform strip along the bottom edge.

**Thumbnail text:** `STILL BROADCASTING`

**Hook:** If you tune a shortwave receiver to 4,625 kilohertz right now, you will hear a
buzz. A short, flat, mechanical tone, repeating about twenty-five times a minute. It has
been doing that, more or less without pause, since the early 1980s. Every so often — and
there is no pattern to it — the buzzing stops, and a voice reads a list of Russian words
and numbers, and then the buzzing starts again.

**Length:** 11 minutes

## 8. The Lake That Exhaled — Lake Nyos, 1986

**Premise:** On one night in August 1986 a Cameroonian crater lake released an invisible
cloud of carbon dioxide that flowed downhill and killed more than seventeen hundred people
in their sleep, and for days nobody could work out what had happened.

**Titles:** 1. The Lake That Killed 1,700 People in One Night · 2. Everyone in the Valley Died
and Nothing Was Damaged · 3. The Night Lake Nyos Exhaled · 4. There Was No Blast. No Fire. No
Survivors in the Valley. · 5. Cameroon, 1986: The Disaster With No Visible Cause

**★ Recommended:** *Everyone in the Valley Died and Nothing Was Damaged* — the intact
buildings are the detail that makes this incomprehensible, and putting it in the title does
the whole job.

**Thumbnail:** Lake Nyos from above, the water an unnatural rust-brown after the event, green
crater walls desaturated around it. Amber arrow tracing the downhill path of the cloud.

**Thumbnail text:** `NOTHING WAS DAMAGED`

**Hook:** On the morning of the twenty-second of August, 1986, the first people to reach the
villages below Lake Nyos found the houses standing, the crops upright, the doors closed. And
almost everyone inside dead. There was no fire, no blast damage, no sign of violence.
Livestock lay in the fields where they had been standing. Insects and birds were dead too.
Whatever had come through the valley had left the buildings entirely alone.

**Length:** 12 minutes · **Note:** clinical handling; a real scientific resolution and a
genuine ongoing risk story makes this both satisfying and important.

## 9. The Lead Masks Case

**Premise:** In 1966, two Brazilian electronics technicians were found dead on a hillside
above Niterói wearing lead masks over their eyes, beside a notebook instructing them to
swallow capsules and wait for a signal.

**Titles:** 1. Two Men, Two Lead Masks, One Note · 2. "Wait for the Agreed Signal" · 3. The
Note Told Them Exactly What to Do · 4. Vintém Hill, 1966: The Lead Masks Case · 5. They Built
the Masks Themselves

**★ Recommended:** *"Wait for the Agreed Signal"* — a real quotation from the notebook. Short,
strange, and it lodges in the memory. Quotation marks in a title signal a primary document,
which is on-brand.

**Thumbnail:** A close, hard-lit still life of a crude hand-cut lead eye mask on dark cloth,
beside a folded notebook page. Amber light raking across the metal. No people.

**Thumbnail text:** `WAIT FOR THE SIGNAL`

**Hook:** On the twentieth of August, 1966, a boy flying a kite on a hill above Niterói, in
Brazil, found two men lying on the ground in formal suits and raincoats. They had been dead
for several days. Each of them was wearing a mask cut from sheet lead, covering the eyes.
Beside them was a notebook. In it, in one of their handwriting, was a short list of
instructions with times attached. The last line said to swallow the capsules and wait for
the agreed signal.

**Length:** 11 minutes

## 10. Wittenoom — The Town That Was Removed From the Map

**Premise:** Australia mined blue asbestos at Wittenoom for twenty years, then spent fifty
years dismantling the town, disconnecting its power, removing it from road signs and
official maps, and finally legislating its last residents out.

**Titles:** 1. Australia Erased This Town From Its Own Maps · 2. Wittenoom Is Still There. It
Is Just Not on Anything. · 3. The Town They Had to Delete · 4. Three Million Tonnes of It Are
Still on the Ground · 5. The Most Contaminated Site in the Southern Hemisphere

**★ Recommended:** *Wittenoom Is Still There. It Is Just Not on Anything.* — the second
sentence reframes the first, and "not on anything" is stranger and more accurate than
"erased."

**Thumbnail:** A road sign with the place name removed, plated over — the blank patch clearly
newer than the metal around it. Red Pilbara dust, hard sun, desaturated to near-monochrome
with the amber accent on the blank plate.

**Thumbnail text:** `REMOVED FROM THE MAP`

**Hook:** If you drive north-east through the Pilbara in Western Australia and follow the
signs, at some point the signs stop mentioning the place you are driving towards. The turn-off
is unmarked. The road is still there. So are the streets, and the foundations, and roughly
three million tonnes of blue asbestos tailings, spread across the valley floor as loose
gravel.

**Length:** 13 minutes

## 11. The Toynbee Tiles

**Premise:** Since the 1980s, hundreds of hand-made tiles bearing the same cryptic message
about resurrecting the dead on Jupiter have appeared embedded in the asphalt of city streets
across the United States and South America, and nobody has ever been charged with laying one.

**Titles:** 1. The Same Message Is Set Into Roads in Twenty Cities · 2. Somebody Has Been
Laying These Tiles for Forty Years · 3. How Do You Put a Tile Into a Road Nobody Saw You
Open? · 4. The Toynbee Tiles · 5. The Message Reached Santiago. Nobody Knows How.

**★ Recommended:** *Somebody Has Been Laying These Tiles for Forty Years* — the duration is
the mystery. It makes the viewer picture a person, which is where the unease lives.

**Thumbnail:** A tile photographed straight down in worn asphalt, block letters visible,
tyre-polished. Amber ring around it, and a faint amber city-grid overlay in the corner
showing the spread.

**Thumbnail text:** `SET INTO THE ROAD`

**Hook:** There is a tile set into the asphalt at an intersection in Philadelphia. It is
about the size of a licence plate, made of layered linoleum, worn almost smooth by traffic.
The text on it, in block capitals, says that a man named Toynbee had an idea, that the idea
appears in the film 2001, and that the dead can be resurrected on the planet Jupiter. There
are hundreds of these. They are in more than twenty cities. Some of them are in South
America.

**Length:** 12 minutes

## 12. The Vela Incident

**Premise:** On 22 September 1979 an American satellite over the South Atlantic recorded the
characteristic double flash of a nuclear detonation, and forty-five years later the United
States has still never officially said whose it was.

**Titles:** 1. A Satellite Saw a Nuclear Flash. No Country Claimed It. · 2. September 1979:
The Double Flash Over the South Atlantic · 3. The Test That Officially Never Happened ·
4. Somebody Set Off a Bomb in 1979 and Nobody Admitted It · 5. Vela 6911 Recorded Something
It Was Built to Recognise

**★ Recommended:** *A Satellite Saw a Nuclear Flash. No Country Claimed It.* — plain, factual,
and the second sentence carries a genuinely enormous implication in five words.

**Thumbnail:** A dark South Atlantic satellite view with a hard amber double-peak waveform
graph overlaid, timestamped. Cold, technical, almost austere — this thumbnail sells rigour,
and rigour is a differentiator.

**Thumbnail text:** `NOBODY CLAIMED IT`

**Hook:** At around one in the morning on the twenty-second of September, 1979, an American
satellite called Vela 6911 recorded a very specific thing over the southern Indian Ocean: a
bright flash, then a dimming, then a second, longer flash. That double pulse is not something
that occurs naturally in that pattern. The satellite existed for the sole purpose of
recognising it. Vela 6911 had seen it forty-one times before, and every one of those had
been a nuclear detonation.

**Length:** 13 minutes · **Note:** stay on the evidence and the panel disagreement; do not
drift into geopolitical advocacy.

## 13. The Hanging Coffins of the Bo

**Premise:** Wooden coffins are wedged into cliff faces up to a hundred metres above the
ground in Sichuan, placed there by a people who were largely destroyed in the sixteenth
century and who left no explanation of how they did it.

**Titles:** 1. Coffins a Hundred Metres Up a Cliff, and No Explanation · 2. The People Who
Put Their Dead in the Sky · 3. Nobody Left a Record of How These Got Up There · 4. The Bo
Disappeared. Their Coffins Didn't. · 5. Sichuan's Cliff Coffins

**★ Recommended:** *The Bo Disappeared. Their Coffins Didn't.* — two four-word sentences, a
whole civilisation and a physical impossibility, and it puts the human loss first.

**Thumbnail:** A vertical limestone cliff face shot from below with dark coffin boxes on
timber pegs high up. Amber scale bar down the left side marking the height. Mist below.

**Thumbnail text:** `100 METRES UP`

**Hook:** On a limestone cliff in Gongxian County, in Sichuan, there are wooden boxes wedged
into the rock face. Some are on timber pegs driven into the stone. Some are on natural
ledges. The highest are around a hundred metres above the valley floor, on vertical rock, with
nothing beneath them. They are coffins. There are hundreds along this stretch of cliff, and
the people who put them there stopped existing as a distinct people around four hundred years
ago.

**Length:** 12 minutes

## 14. The Max Headroom Intrusion

**Premise:** On the evening of 22 November 1987 somebody with broadcast-grade transmitting
equipment hijacked two Chicago television stations within two hours, transmitted ninety
seconds of incoherent video, and was never identified.

**Titles:** 1. Two TV Stations Were Hijacked in One Night. Nobody Was Caught. · 2. Chicago,
1987: Ninety Seconds That Nobody Can Explain · 3. Whoever Did This Had Broadcast Equipment ·
4. The Signal Intrusion of November 22nd · 5. It Happened Twice, Two Hours Apart

**★ Recommended:** *Whoever Did This Had Broadcast Equipment* — sidesteps the meme the story
usually gets reduced to and reframes it as a technical crime, which is both the fresher angle
and the accurate one.

**Thumbnail:** A CRT screen photographed off-axis in a dark room, showing heavy analogue
distortion and scan lines, no recognisable figure. Amber timestamp overlay `21:14 CST`.

**Thumbnail text:** `TWO STATIONS. ONE NIGHT.`

**Hook:** At about nine in the evening on the twenty-second of November, 1987, the sports
report on Chicago's Channel Nine cut out. For roughly half a minute the screen showed
something else, with no sound the station could hear. The engineers switched to a different
transmission link and got the signal back. Two hours later, on a different station, on a
different frequency, it happened again — and that time it lasted a minute and a half.

**Length:** 11 minutes

## 15. Tromelin — Fifteen Years on a Sandbank

**Premise:** In 1761 a French ship wrecked on a half-mile sandbank in the Indian Ocean; the
crew built a boat and left, promising to send help for the sixty enslaved Malagasy people
they left behind, and when a ship finally came fifteen years later, seven women and a baby
walked out to meet it.

**Titles:** 1. They Were Left on a Sandbank for Fifteen Years · 2. Tromelin: The Island Half a
Mile Across · 3. Eight People Were Still Alive When the Ship Came Back · 4. The Rescue That
Took Fifteen Years to Arrive · 5. Archaeologists Found What They Built

**★ Recommended:** *Eight People Were Still Alive When the Ship Came Back* — the survival is
the story, the number does the emotional work, and it centres the people who were abandoned
rather than the people who abandoned them.

**Thumbnail:** An aerial of Tromelin — a pale sliver of sand ringed by reef in enormous open
ocean, almost lost in the frame. Amber scale bar showing 1,700 metres across. Nothing else.

**Thumbnail text:** `FIFTEEN YEARS HERE`

**Hook:** Tromelin is a sandbank in the Indian Ocean, about a mile and a half long and half a
mile across. There is no fresh water and there are no trees. In July 1761 a French merchant
ship ran onto its reef in the dark. The crew got ashore, built a boat from the wreckage, and
sailed away, leaving behind roughly sixty enslaved Malagasy people they had been transporting
illegally. They said they would send a ship. The first one arrived in 1776.

**Length:** 14 minutes · **Note:** a candidate for the quarterly flagship. The 2006 French
archaeological excavation of the survivors' stone structures gives it a genuine third act and
extraordinary visual material.

## 16. Unknown Man E and the Harem Conspiracy

**Premise:** A body found in a royal Egyptian cache in 1881 was wrapped in ritually impure
sheepskin, left unnamed, and mummified with its mouth open — and a three-thousand-year-old
trial transcript may explain exactly who he was and what he did.

**Titles:** 1. He Was Buried in a Way Designed to Punish Him · 2. The Unnamed Mummy and the
3,000-Year-Old Trial Record · 3. Somebody Made Sure This Man Had No Afterlife · 4. Unknown
Man E · 5. The Assassination of Ramesses III, and the Body Nobody Would Name

**★ Recommended:** *He Was Buried in a Way Designed to Punish Him* — it establishes intent,
which is far more interesting than the physical strangeness, and it makes the viewer ask what
he had done.

**Thumbnail:** A museum photograph of the wrapped mummy in low raking light, framed so the
face is turned away and only the sheepskin wrapping and the shape are legible. Amber
underline on a line of hieroglyphic text composited beside it.

**Thumbnail text:** `NO NAME. NO AFTERLIFE.`

**Hook:** When the royal cache at Deir el-Bahari was opened in 1881, it contained the bodies
of pharaohs — carefully prepared, named, wrapped in fine linen. One of them was none of those
things. He had been wrapped in a sheepskin, which in ancient Egyptian practice was
ritually unclean. His organs had not been removed. No name was written anywhere on or near
him. And his mouth was wide open.

**Length:** 12 minutes

## 17. The Waratah

**Premise:** A 500-foot passenger liner with 211 people aboard sailed from Durban in July
1909, was seen once the following morning, and then vanished so completely that a century of
searching has not produced a single confirmed fragment of her.

**Titles:** 1. A 500-Foot Liner With 211 People Aboard, and Not One Piece Has Been Found ·
2. The Waratah Sailed From Durban and Was Never Seen Again · 3. The Largest Ship Ever to
Simply Disappear · 4. They Have Been Looking for This Wreck for a Hundred Years · 5. One
Passenger Got Off Early Because of a Dream

**★ Recommended:** *A 500-Foot Liner With 211 People Aboard, and Not One Piece Has Been
Found* — long, but the accumulation of specifics is what makes it land, and the last clause
is the impossible part.

**Thumbnail:** A period photograph of Waratah at anchor, high contrast, with an amber search-
grid overlaid across the sea in front of her — decades of survey boxes, all empty.

**Thumbnail text:** `NOT ONE FRAGMENT`

**Hook:** The Waratah left Durban on the twenty-sixth of July, 1909, bound for Cape Town. She
was five hundred feet long, less than two years old, and carried two hundred and eleven
people. At dawn the next morning another ship passed her off the Transkei coast and exchanged
signals. That was the last time anybody saw her. In the hundred and fifteen years since, the
searches have found several wrecks off that coastline. None of them has been the Waratah.

**Length:** 12 minutes · **Note:** the passenger who disembarked at Durban after a recurring
dream is documented as a contemporary account — tag it `REPORTED`, and resist the temptation
to make it the spine.

## 18. The Yuba County Five

**Premise:** In February 1978 five men left a basketball game in northern California and drove
into the mountains for no reason anyone has ever established, abandoning a working car on a
snowbound forest road; four were eventually found, and what one of them did before he died
makes less sense than the disappearance itself.

**Titles:** 1. The Car Was Fine. The Road Went Nowhere. · 2. Five Men Drove Into the Mountains
for No Reason · 3. He Survived for Weeks in a Trailer Full of Food He Never Opened · 4. Yuba
County, 1978 · 5. Nothing About the Route Makes Sense

**★ Recommended:** *The Car Was Fine. The Road Went Nowhere.* — two short factual sentences
that eliminate the obvious explanation before the video starts. Thirty-eight characters.

**Thumbnail:** A snowbound forest service road at dusk, tyre tracks ending, dense pine
either side. An amber marker where the car was found. No vehicle in shot — the absence is the
image.

**Thumbnail text:** `SIXTY MILES THE WRONG WAY`

**Hook:** On the night of the twenty-fourth of February, 1978, five men drove home from a
college basketball game in Chico, California. Home was south. Four days later their car was
found seventy miles east, high in the Plumas National Forest, on a snow-covered mountain
road it had no business being on. The car was undamaged. It started first time. There was
enough fuel in the tank to get back down.

**Length:** 13 minutes · **Note:** four of the five men had developmental disabilities or
mental illness. Write with care and without condescension; this is a story about how a search
was conducted, not a story about strangeness.

## 19. The Devil's Footprints

**Premise:** On a February morning in 1855, people across a hundred miles of Devon woke to a
single line of hoof-shaped prints in fresh snow, running over roofs, walls, haystacks and a
frozen estuary, and the Victorian press could not agree on an explanation any more than we can.

**Titles:** 1. One Line of Prints, a Hundred Miles Across Devon · 2. They Went Over the
Rooftops · 3. February 1855: The Morning Devon Woke Up to This · 4. The Prints Crossed a
Two-Mile Estuary · 5. Nothing Walks in a Perfectly Straight Line

**★ Recommended:** *They Went Over the Rooftops* — five words, no context, physically
impossible. The thumbnail supplies the rest. This is packaging working as a pair.

**Thumbnail:** A snow-covered Victorian rooftop at dawn, a single line of dark hoof-shaped
prints crossing the ridge tiles and continuing. Amber-lit windows below. Labelled
`RECREATION` per the visual rules.

**Thumbnail text:** `OVER THE ROOFTOPS`

**Hook:** It snowed across south Devon on the night of the eighth of February, 1855. In the
morning, people in more than thirty parishes found the same thing in the fresh snow outside
their houses: a single line of small hoof-shaped prints, about four inches long, spaced
evenly, in single file. Not a wandering track. A line. It went across gardens and fields, and
then over walls, and then across rooftops, and continued on the other side.

**Length:** 11 minutes · **Note:** the contemporary *Times* and *Illustrated London News*
coverage is the primary record and it is genuinely rich; the competing Victorian explanations
are the best part of the episode. Keep the `REPORTED` tag up almost throughout.

## 20. The Bell Island Boom

**Premise:** On 2 April 1978 an enormous explosion tore holes in the ground on an island in
Newfoundland, damaged a house, and killed chickens in a coop, and within days two scientists
from Los Alamos had arrived to look at it.

**Titles:** 1. Something Exploded Over a Newfoundland Island in 1978 · 2. It Blew Holes in the
Ground and Nobody Agrees Why · 3. Two Los Alamos Scientists Arrived Within Days · 4. Bell
Island, April 1978 · 5. The Boom That Was Heard Across the Bay

**★ Recommended:** *Two Los Alamos Scientists Arrived Within Days* — the response is more
interesting than the event, and it is a documented fact rather than an insinuation, which
lets you handle the story straight.

**Thumbnail:** A shallow blast crater in frozen Newfoundland ground, torn earth, a hand-scale
object for reference. Grey sea and cliff in the background. Amber ring around the hole.

**Thumbnail text:** `WHAT MADE THIS HOLE?`

**Hook:** At around eleven in the morning on the second of April, 1978, people across
Conception Bay in Newfoundland heard an explosion. On Bell Island, in the middle of the bay,
it did real damage — it blew out the front of a house, tore two holes in the frozen ground,
and killed a coop of chickens. There was no aircraft overhead, no industrial site nearby, and
no storm in the immediate area. Within a few days, two researchers from Los Alamos National
Laboratory were on the island.

**Length:** 11 minutes · **Note:** the researchers were studying an atmospheric phenomenon,
and the honest version of this episode says so clearly. The interest is that the leading
explanation is itself barely understood.

# Bench — three reserves

- **Frederick Valentich, 1978.** A pilot over Bass Strait describes an object to Melbourne
  air traffic control, says "it is not an aircraft," and the recording ends in seventeen
  seconds of metallic noise. Strong, but the transcript does most of the work and the
  audience skews toward the UFO community you are not targeting. Use it once you can afford
  the tone control.
- **The 52-Hertz Whale.** A single animal in the North Pacific that has been calling at a
  frequency no other whale uses, tracked since 1989. Melancholy rather than dark — an
  excellent palate-cleanser between two heavy episodes, and a strong candidate for a Short.
- **Kayaköy / the erased villages.** A category rather than a story; useful when you need a
  place-driven episode at short notice.

# Do not commission

Ideas that fail the source test, kept here so you don't re-litigate them at 11pm:

- **The SS Ourang Medan.** The entire evidentiary chain runs back to a small number of
  mid-century publications with no supporting record. Not an unexplained event. It *is* a
  good episode about how an untraceable story propagates — that framing only.
- **"The man from Taured."** Same problem, worse. Only usable as a case study in
  fabrication.
- **The Anjikuni "vanished village."** Documented as a fabrication by the RCMP.
- Anything whose central claim you can only find in blog posts and other YouTube videos.
- Anything requiring you to speculate about a living named private individual's guilt.
