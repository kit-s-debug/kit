01 — The Club

More than a
rugby club

Same village. Same colours. One hundred and forty seasons.

Llangwm is a small village on the Daugleddau, a few miles south of Haverfordwest. It has had a rugby team since 1885.

That is most of the story. Generation after generation of the same families have pulled on the black and amber, played their Saturday and walked back up to the clubhouse afterwards. Read the names on the 1909 team photograph — Jones, Morgan, John, Bryant — and you are reading names still called across the touchline today.

Today the club runs a First XV and a mini and junior section, and the clubhouse on The Green is still where the village meets. New players, new supporters and new volunteers are always wanted. You do not need to have played before, and you do not need to be from here.

05 — Club identity

Why the
Wasps?

Black and amber, hooped, and unmistakable on a wet February field. The nickname comes straight off the shirt, and it has stuck so firmly that the club's own match reports use nothing else — a good afternoon gets written up as the Wasps stinging somebody.

The surviving history does not record the season the colours were first pulled on. What it does record is how long they have lasted. Long enough that black and amber is simply what Llangwm looks like.

06 — Our history

1885
to today

Almost everything known about the club's first hundred years survives because one man wrote it down. Richard Howells' centenary history, A Hundred Years of Rugby 1885–1985, is long out of print, but the Llangwm Local History Society has preserved its records online. What follows is drawn from that work.

  1. 1885

    The club is founded

    Rugby takes root in the village. A century later the club's centenary is marked by Richard Howells' history — the book from which most of what we know about the early years survives.

  2. 1909

    Gold medals at Pembroke Dock

    In April a Llangwm side returns from the Pembroke Dock tournament with gold medals, captained by J. Adams. The photograph names more than twenty players and officials — Joneses, Morgans, Johns and Bryants.

  3. 1910s

    A village side on the record

    Team photographs from 1910–11 and 1913–14 show a settled First XV, with names including Teddy Morgan, Albert Palmer, Willie John and Jack Morgan.

  4. 1920–21

    Runners-up, West of Llanelly Junior League

    Rugby returns after the First World War and Llangwm go close in the West of Llanelly Junior League, scoring 256 points against 57 — the first of the club's serious runs at league honours.

  5. 1923–24

    League champions

    Llangwm take the league title under captain W. Palmer, with Lieut-Col. Lort Phillips as club president and T. Phillips as chairman. The club's first recorded championship.

  6. 1925–26

    West of Llanelli League champions

    A second title inside three seasons confirms the side as a force in west Wales junior rugby.

  7. 1930s

    Sir Charles, Lady Price, and a point short

    The 1935–36 club photograph has Sir Charles W. M. Price as president and Lady Price as a life member, with the chairman of the Union of Welsh Junior League down from Cardiff to sit in it. In 1937 the village hosts a Cardiff District side. In 1938 Llangwm go out of the first round of the K.O. Cup to Whitland by 4 points to 3.

  8. 1946–47

    League Cup winners

    The first silverware of the post-war years, Ken Adams captaining the side. That December, seven Llangwm men line up for the Pembrokeshire County XV alongside players from Neyland, Narberth and Tenby.

  9. 1948–49

    Pembrokeshire K.O. Cup winners

    Cup winners and league runners-up in the same season — the beginning of Llangwm's strongest era.

  10. 1949–50

    Bishop Cup winners

    Back-to-back cup seasons, and the Bishop Cup comes to the village.

  11. 1956–57

    League champions again

    G. Vaughan captains a title-winning side, with James John as president and E. Jones as chairman. The photograph shows the league trophy and a row of small cups in front of it.

  12. 1958–59

    Pembrokeshire Junior K.O. Cup winners

    Mel Scale captains the side to the Junior Knock-Out Cup, with Ald. J. John as president and R. V. Palmer as chairman, alongside players including John Brock, K. Roberts, D. Hughes, D. Harvard and T. Philpin.

  13. 1959–60

    The double

    League and Knock Out Cup winners of the Pembrokeshire and District Rugby Union in the same season — two cups and a row of trophies on the grass in front of the whole club.

  14. 1984

    America comes to Llangwm

    On 8 September the Eastern Rugby Union of America's 50th anniversary tour of Wales plays a match at Llangwm. A touring international side, on a village field.

  15. 1985

    The centenary

    One hundred years of rugby in Llangwm, recorded by Richard Howells in the history that keeps the club's first century readable.

  16. Today

    Still the Wasps

    A First XV, a mini and junior section, and a clubhouse at the heart of the village. Black and amber, as it has been for generations.

09 — Mini & junior

Start here

The junior section is where most Wasps start. Age groups play across Pembrokeshire against clubs including Narberth, Fishguard & Goodwick and St Clears & Whitland.

No experience needed, no kit needed for a first session, and parents are as welcome to get involved as the players. Come down, have a look, see if it suits.
